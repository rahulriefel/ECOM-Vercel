GST and compliance basics for new online sellers in India

RMBy Rahul Mishra · 31 August 2026 · 7 min read

The paperwork side of selling online trips up more new brands than the selling itself. This is a plain-English overview to help you know what to expect — it is general information, not tax or legal advice, so confirm the specifics for your business with a qualified CA.

GST registration

Most marketplaces in India require a GST registration to sell taxable goods, regardless of turnover, because you are supplying through an e-commerce operator. Getting your GSTIN sorted early is usually the first unblocker — it is also the step most likely to delay a launch, so start it before everything else.

Getting your details consistent

Marketplaces verify your business name, GSTIN, PAN and bank details, and they must match. Small mismatches (a comma in a business name, an old address) cause avoidable rejections. Keep one accurate set of details and use it everywhere.

Records to keep from day one

  • Invoices and tax records for every order, in the format your accountant needs for GST returns.
  • Settlement and fee reports from each marketplace — these reconcile what you actually received against what you sold.
  • Purchase and inventory records so your input credits and cost of goods are clean.

Other registrations you may encounter

Depending on what and where you sell, you might come across things like an import-export code for cross-border, trademark for brand protection and Brand Registry, and category-specific certifications. Not all apply to everyone — the point is to check early rather than discover a blocker mid-launch.

Why it is worth getting right

Clean compliance is not just about avoiding penalties. Accurate records make your numbers trustworthy, your GST returns painless, and your business easier to run and eventually sell. Sloppy paperwork, on the other hand, quietly costs you input credits and creates stress at filing time.
