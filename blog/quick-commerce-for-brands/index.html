Quick commerce for brands: Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart

RMBy Rahul Mishra · 19 October 2026 · 7 min read

Quick commerce — 10-to-30-minute delivery through platforms like Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart — has changed how India buys everyday products. For the right brand it is a powerful new channel; for the wrong one it is a distraction. Here is how to tell.

How it differs from regular marketplaces

Quick commerce runs on dark stores — small local warehouses stocked with a tight range of fast-moving products. That has big implications: shelf space is limited, so platforms are selective, and your product has to sell through quickly to keep its slot. It rewards high-frequency, impulse and convenience purchases.

Which products fit

  • Great fit: food and beverage, personal care, household essentials, and anything bought on impulse or in a hurry.
  • Weaker fit: considered, high-ticket or highly variable-SKU products that need browsing and comparison.

What winning looks like

Because range is limited, getting listed is competitive and often involves margin and marketing commitments. Once in, velocity is king — you keep your slot by selling through. Packaging that survives fast handling and communicates instantly on a small screen matters more here than almost anywhere.

Should you invest?

If your product is a frequent, convenience-led buy, quick commerce can add real incremental sales on top of your marketplace and D2C channels. If it is a considered purchase, put your energy elsewhere first. A free audit will tell you honestly whether it is worth the effort for your catalogue.
